# LinkedIn · Carousel · Too Many Musicians, Not Enough Who Still Love Music · v3 · 2026-10-30

> **Publish:** LinkedIn document/carousel · 4 slides + long caption · images in `ceo-inbox/assets/2026-10-30-musicians-linkedin/`

## Slide 1 · Supply vs seats

**Headline:** Conservatories scale output. Auditions don’t.

**Body:** You can practice until your hands shake—then a buyer asks: “How many players? Can you keep it quiet for cocktails?” That’s not a music brief. That’s wallpaper.

**Image:** `linkedin-slide-01-supply-demand.jpg`


## Slide 3 · T-shaped musician · business cognition

**Headline:** Depth + four literacies (no MBA cosplay)

**Body:** One real artistic peak + enough commercial sense to read a brief, respect flow, ship a 15s clip, and sign a contract. For teammates who “only play”: one-page onboarding, split roles, no shame—structure.

**Image:** `linkedin-slide-03-business-cognition.jpg`


## Long caption (paste under carousel)

I’ve played the gig where the strings live behind a column and the client’s goal is “feel expensive.” I’ve also played where music is exhibit #0 on the route. Same dress code. Opposite outcomes.

Conservatories graduate more musicians than the orchestra market can seat—that’s math. What hurts more is **hiring musicians to pretend you care about art** while the brief only specifies headcount and Canon.

Three questions I ask before I say yes (steal them):

1. Cocktail or **story**—which one are we buying?  
2. Is there a **15-second moment** PR can actually post?  
3. After teardown, can your staff **repeat one sentence** about what the music did?

If you’re planning a launch or exhibition and want music in the journey—not the corner—DM **“brief”** with one line on your scene.
